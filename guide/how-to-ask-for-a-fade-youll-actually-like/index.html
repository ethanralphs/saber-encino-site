How to Ask for a Fade You'll Actually Like

Published October 5, 2026 · 3 min read

Most guys who walk in asking for a fade already have a picture in their head. The tricky part is getting that picture out of your head and into the barber's hands. "Just a fade" means about fifty different things depending on who you ask, so a little specificity goes a long way — and it doesn't have to be technical. Here's what actually helps.

Two things to nail down before you sit down

The first is where the fade starts. A high fade begins close to the temples and crown, leaving a shorter overall silhouette. A mid fade starts around the top of the ear — that's probably the most common one we do here at the shop on Ventura. A low fade keeps most of the length and just cleans up the sides from the bottom, which works great if you want to keep some weight and structure. If you're not sure which one, just point to your head and say "about here" — that's genuinely enough to start the conversation.

The second thing is how sharp you want the blend. A skin fade goes all the way down to bare skin at the bottom. A drop fade follows the natural curve behind the ear for a slightly softer look. If you want something more subtle, a taper just gradually blends without going completely bare. None of these are hard to ask for — just say "skin fade," "drop fade," or "tapered."

If you have a photo, use it. Seriously. Show it to us right when you sit down. You don't have to explain anything beyond that — a good barber will look at it, ask one or two clarifying questions, and take it from there. The best photos are ones where the lighting is decent and the angle shows both sides of the head.

What to say if you're not sure what you want

This happens more than you'd think, and it's completely fine. The easiest starting point is to describe what you don't like about your current cut. "It's getting too bushy on the sides" or "the back looks sloppy" tells us exactly where to focus without needing a vocabulary lesson. From there we can suggest something that fits your hair texture and how much upkeep you actually want to do at home.

One thing worth mentioning: if you're on the fence about going shorter, start conservative. You can always take more off — you can't put it back. Most guys who say "just take a little off" and then decide they want it tighter just come back a few weeks later, which is honestly not a bad system.

If you want to check us out before committing, walk-ins are always welcome — or you can check in online so you're not waiting around when you get here. Either way works.

The whole point of a good consultation is that you shouldn't feel like you have to know the right terminology. Tell us what you're going for in plain language, show us a photo if you've got one, and we'll handle the rest.
