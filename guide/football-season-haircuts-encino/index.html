Game-Day Ready: The Best Haircuts for Football Season in Encino

Published July 25, 2026 · 4 min read

By late August, you can feel it up and down the Ventura Blvd corridor — the pads come back on, the Friday night lights come up, and Crespi Carmelite football has the Celts chasing another strong season. Whether you're the one suiting up or the one in the stands, football season has a way of sneaking up on you. A sharp, no-fuss haircut is one of the easiest ways to feel ready for it.

Why a football-season cut is different

A cut that has to look clean Friday night under the lights and survive a helmet, a chin strap, and a soaked practice jersey by Monday is a different ask than a regular haircut. Long, floppy styles that need product and a mirror to sit right just don't hold up — one two-a-day and they're flat, stuck to your forehead, and hanging in your eyes. The goal for the season is a cut that looks intentional with zero effort and grows out cleanly, so you're not back in the chair every single week.

The best low-maintenance cuts for student athletes

Three cuts do the heavy lifting all season long:

  • Skin fade or taper fade — clean lines, nothing to fuss with, and it grows out evenly so it still looks sharp weeks later. The tightest, most helmet-friendly option there is.
  • Buzz cut — zero maintenance, full stop. If you're deep in two-a-days and don't want to think about your hair at all, this is the one. Wake up and go.
  • Textured crop — a little length left up top so you've got something to style on game day, kept short on the sides so it stays out of the way under a helmet. The best of both worlds if you don't want to go super short.

Not sure which way to go? Tell your Stylist your position and how often you practice, and they'll steer you to the right length.

For the dads and coaches on the sideline

Game day isn't just for the players. If you're coaching, running the chains, or just cheering from the fence every Friday, looking sharp on the sideline counts too. Make it a full visit with the MVP Experience — a precision cut finished with a hot steamed towel, so you walk out camera-ready for the season opener.

Timing it right

The move is to book your cut a week or two before the season opener — early-to-mid August — not the night before. That gives a fresh fade a day or two to settle so it looks natural on the field, and it keeps you out of the back-to-school rush, when every chair in Encino seems to fill up at once.
